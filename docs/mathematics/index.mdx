# Mathematics

The mathematics in this part is selected by a single criterion: it must be needed to state
what a component computes, or to bound the error in what it actually computes.

## The role of this part

A generic numerical component has a domain, and that domain is almost always an algebraic
structure. A summation algorithm needs an associative operation with an identity; a
Gram–Schmidt orthogonalisation needs an inner product space over an ordered field; a
conjugate-gradient iteration needs a symmetric positive-definite operator and the field
arithmetic to form Krylov combinations. These requirements are mathematical statements before
they are C++ concepts, and stating them first is what keeps the concepts honest.

The second role is error. Every computation in floating-point arithmetic solves a nearby
problem, and the distance between that problem and the intended one is governed by the
conditioning of the problem and the stability of the algorithm. This is not a caveat attached
to numerical entries; it is part of their specification, and it is developed in
[Numerical Analysis](/docs/mathematics/numerical-analysis).

## Notation

Notation follows [Conventions](/docs/conventions). In brief: $F$ is a field, $V$ a vector
space over $F$, $A \in F^{m \times n}$ a matrix, $u$ the unit roundoff, and $\hat{x}$ a
computed approximation to $x$.

## How structure becomes an interface

A structure is a carrier set with operations and laws. The translation into C++ is
mechanical for the carrier and the operations, and impossible for the laws:

$$
(R, +, \cdot, 0, 1) \quad\longmapsto\quad \texttt{concept Ring} = \text{operations only}.
$$

The laws — associativity, distributivity, the identities — are recorded as semantic
requirements and go unchecked. This is not a deficiency to be worked around but a fact to be
managed, and the discipline for managing it is in
[Semantic Requirements](/docs/formal-cpp/semantic-requirements).

The floating-point case is worth stating at the outset because it recurs in every entry:
`double` with addition is not a monoid, since addition is not associative. Every algorithm
whose correctness argument uses associativity — every parallel reduction, every vectorized
summation — therefore computes something other than the mathematical sum, and the difference
is bounded rather than absent.
